# polymathicX/Agun-Panjika — code teardown (31 Oct 2026)

Team KUETaguns (KUET, Bangladesh). Repo HEAD 289221826c2f, last push 29 Oct 2026. Read as text only via `gh api`; nothing cloned or run. Local copies of the files read are in `competitors/scratch/ap/`.

## 1. Harmonization as implemented

- **Direction:** MODIS -> VIIRS **S-NPP** scale (`pipeline/harmonise.py` module docstring L1-24). The output series is: observed filtered S-NPP count whenever S-NPP flew; model estimate from MODIS otherwise (2001-2011 and S-NPP gaps); NOAA-20/21 x a bridge factor after the S-NPP end (`pipeline/climatology.py` L5-8, L65-109).
- **Space/time unit:** the model is trained on **region-weeks**. There are 5 hand-drawn Bangladesh analysis regions, and the time unit is the ISO week (`harmonise.py` L59-93, `aggregate.py` L33-73). The national value is the sum of the regions. Districts (64) are **not modelled**: the region-week estimate is split by each district's MODIS count that week, or by its S-NPP climatological share for that month when MODIS saw nothing (`harmonise.py` L562-581). Upazila and union calendars are apportioned further from that (`pipeline/local.py`). There is no lat/lon grid.
- **Models compared:** fixed 4.4x; one factor refit per fold; 12 monthly factors; NB2 GLM `log mu = a[region,month] + b*log1p(M) + g*log1p(F)`, fitted by IRLS with alpha from a profile likelihood, sparse cells pooled into "quiet" intercepts (`harmonise.py` L160-263); a 5-seed bagged Poisson LightGBM (500 trees, lr 0.03, 15 leaves) on 25 features (`GBM_COLS` L42-48, L266-296). **Shipped model = 0.3*NB + 0.7*GBM** (`WEIGHT_NB=0.3` L49, L299-312).
- **GBM features:** MODIS count, FRP, Aqua/Terra split, night share, low-FRP (<15 MW) share, high-confidence count, number of burning districts, `lM_prev`, **`lM_next` (next week's MODIS count)**, a 4-week rolling mean, month, ISO week, NASA POWER cloud, precipitation and RH, FWI and DC, and MODIS view geometry (`pix_mean`, `nadir_share`, `pix_nat`). The NB gets only `log1p(M)` and `log1p(F)`. Fitted final model: b=0.777, g=0.031, alpha=0.51, 50 intercepts (`web/public/data/validation.json` `final_fit`). The top GBM split importances are fwi, lM_4, cloud, lM_next and rh, so weather drives as many splits as MODIS does.
- **Filters** (`pipeline/kilns.py`): MODIS confidence >=30; VIIRS nominal or high only (L58-59); FIRMS type 2/3 dropped. A persistent-heat cell filter uses a 0.0036 deg (~400 m) grid **defined from VIIRS detections across all years**. A cell is flagged when it has >=4 distinct months, >=3 years, >=10 detections, <50% of detections in the March-April jhum peak and >=50% at night (L29-51). MODIS detections are masked if any flagged cell lies in their 3x3 neighbourhood (L54-74). Precision was spot-checked by hand in 3 rounds of 20 cells (`docs/kiln_spotcheck_round*.csv`). Day/night is used as a feature and in the kiln rule, not as a stratum.
- **Scan angle:** used only as features (`pix_km2 = scan*track`, `ingest.py` L135; nadir share `aggregate.py` L44). There is no physical operator.
- **NOAA-20/21** (`pipeline/bridge.py`): three forms (identity, single factor, monthly factors shrunk with tau=300) are compared by LOYO on the national weekly overlap with S-NPP. NOAA-20 uses 2019-2025 and NOAA-21 uses 2024-2026 from the API. Both select **identity**. Successors are used only after the hard-coded S-NPP end `2026-11-02T13:00Z` or in S-NPP outage weeks. In those weeks the value is the **mean** of the available bridged NOAA-20/21 counts (`climatology.py` L46-50, L93-100). Their own review notes that `local.py` and `neighbours.py` bypass the bridge factors (`docs/SCIENTIFIC_REVIEW.md` item 4).
- **Terra/Aqua:** there is a separate **Terra-only model** for 2001 to ISO 2002-W26 (`TerraModel`, `harmonise.py` L315-332; `AQUA_START` in `config.py` L68), and `aqua_share` is a feature. **No explicit orbital-drift handling**: training runs through 2025 and covers the drift years. UNVERIFIED observation: in `partner.json` MODIS 2022 = 866 against S-NPP 6,026 (ratio 7.0), where the typical ratio is about 3.5.
- **Overlap/training period:** `OVERLAP_YEARS = 2012..2025` (`config.py` L64), excluding ISO 2012 W1-3 (`harmonise.py` L110-114). The final model is fit on all 14 years (`harmonise.py` L598-599).

## 2. Validation and leakage

- **Split:** leave-one-year-out over the 14 overlap years at region-week level (`loyo_predictions`, L431-440). Metrics are computed on the **national** weekly series per held-out year: MAE, off-season MAE, annual total % error, peak-week error and year-over-year direction match (L415-488).
- **Reported:** ensemble national weekly MAE **36.964** vs refit single factor **49.450** (-25.3%) vs fixed 4.4x 60.97. GBM alone scores 38.68 and the NB 44.11. Annual total error is 9.5% vs 12.8%.
- **Reproduced:** (a) my script `scratch/scripts/ap_check.py` recomputes the means from `per_year`: 36.964 / 49.449 / 38.684. (b) The **31 Oct 2026 GitHub Actions run 41197112994** refit the whole pipeline from FIRMS and logged ensemble 36.967 vs naive 49.448 and coverage 0.803 / 0.816. The numbers reproduce from the raw data. The ensemble is worse than the single factor in held-out years **2021 and 2024** (national), and in some region-years in the CI log (for example 2014 and 2021).
- **Leakage / optimism found:**
  1. **Model and feature selection on the test years.** `WEIGHT_NB = 0.3`, the GBM hyperparameters, the 25-feature set and `SHIPPED="ensemble"` (`harmonise.py` L42-51) were chosen while looking at the same LOYO scores that are published. There is no untouched final holdout. The team admits this (`docs/SCIENTIFIC_REVIEW.md` item 3).
  2. **The interval calibration is not nested.** `interval_coverage` (L495-524) leaves year y out of the conformal calibration set, but the out-of-fold residuals of the other years come from models trained with y included. The team admits this (review item 2). The effect is mild, CV+-style.
  3. **The static-source cells are learned from all years**, including the test years (`kilns.py` L29-51, `main` L97-107). The cells are defined from VIIRS, which is the target sensor, and then used to mask MODIS inputs. This is a mild target-derived preprocessing leak.
  4. **`lM_next`** uses next week's MODIS. That is fine for reconstruction but means the method is not real-time. It is disclosed (README).
  5. The **ESA burned-area "jump" test** (`partner.json` `jump_2012`: 0.95 harmonized vs 3.09 stitched) is weak evidence. MODIS-only gives 0.81, so the test mainly shows that naive stitching is wrong. Post-2012 "harmonized" values *are* the observed S-NPP. I reproduced 0.949 and 0.806 from `partner.json`.
  6. The district, upazila and union series are never validated, because they are apportioned.

## 3. Uncertainty and calibration

- **Method:** log-residual split-conformal quantiles at 80% (q10/q90), with the finite-sample correction `ceil((n+1)p)`, stratified by 5 predicted-activity bins x peak/off-season (`ConformalBands`, L353-393). A separate annual-total band uses held-out annual log errors per unit (`AnnualBands`, L396-409). NOAA-bridged weeks get their own bands from the overlap (`climatology.py` L53-62). There are posterior-predictive NB draws (`NegBinModel.sample`, L251-258), but they are **not used** for the shipped bands.
- **Calibration is checked in code:** empirical coverage for a nominal 80% band is **80.1% region-week, 81.3% national-week, 85.7% national-year and region-year** (`validation.json` `interval_coverage`; reproduced by CI as 80.3% / 81.6%). The median national-week band width is 1.39x the estimate, so the bands are wide. Only the 80% level is tested. There is no 95% band and no PIT histogram.
- **Gap:** seasonal percentiles and alert levels (`climatology.py` L117-161; levels 25/75/90; "quiet" when the median is <3) use the **point estimate only**. The interval is not propagated into "is this season above normal". That is CEDRUS's opening.

## 4. Web app

- **Stack:** Vite 5 + React 18 + TypeScript + Tailwind, d3, maplibre-gl 4, three.js 0.186 (a textured globe), i18next (English/Bangla), and html-to-image for the PNG share card (`web/package.json`). Fully static: it reads committed JSON and CSV from `web/public/data/` (~13 MB in total). There is a service worker for offline use (`web/public/sw.js`). Their own perf record shows about 350 KB of decoded JS and LCP about 0.55 s on localhost (`docs/upgrade-progress/performance.json`).
- **Screens:** Home (globe plus seasonal status), Panjika (year x ISO-week calendar heatmap), Sensors (raw vs factor vs harmonized, monthly ratios, validation, **sonification**), Map (district choropleth, yearly hotspot points, kiln toggle, table view), Outlook (experimental 2-week LightGBM outlook, FWI), Borders (CHT vs Tripura/Mizoram/Chin, Dhaka AOD), Method (downloads, citation) (`web/src/screens/*.tsx`). There is a guided demo tour (`components/DemoTour.tsx`), an RSS/CAP feed (`web/public/data/alerts*.xml`), and print and PNG export.
- **Area selection:** **a dropdown of predefined units only.** The units are national, 5 regions and 64 districts (`components/RegionPicker.tsx`), plus clicking a district on the map (`MapScreen.tsx` L201-211). There is **no drawing, bbox or lat/lon entry**. The README says outright that arbitrary polygons are not supported.
- **CSV:** two **static pre-built files**, linked from the Method screen (`screens/Method.tsx` L136-139). The first is `download/harmonised.csv` (0.5 MB), with columns unit_type, unit_id, iso_year, iso_week, modis_count, viirs_snpp_count, viirs_noaa20_count, naive_scaled, harmonised, lo80, hi80, source. The second is `harmonised_districts.csv` (5.2 MB). **These are harmonized counts only. There is no raw-hotspot CSV and no per-selection export.** Map points are served as quantized per-year JSON arrays (`points/{year}.json`) and are not downloadable.
- **Accessibility:** this is the strongest of any repo I have seen. The calendar is an ARIA `role="grid"` with rowcount/colcount, `aria-activedescendant`, arrow-key navigation and per-cell `aria-label` (`components/CalendarHeatmap.tsx` L80-133). There is a table alternative, a WCAG AA contrast unit test (`tests/test_contrast.py`), `prefers-reduced-motion` handling (`MapScreen.tsx` L197), and a 42-view axe matrix with 0 findings (`docs/review/verification-2026-10-29.json`). The ramp is a single-hue orange sequential palette (`web/src/index.css` L51-55, monotone lightness), so it is colour-blind-safe by lightness. There is no explicit CVD simulation test.

## 5. Weaknesses a judge would notice

1. No working public demo (404). Every daily deploy fails.
2. Bangladesh only. Nepal is a config file with no validation (`pipeline/countries/npl.json`). The calendar is national, region or district, not a user-drawn area as the challenge asks.
3. The CSV holds harmonized aggregates, not "downloadable CSV of hotspots".
4. The model is heavily engineered (GBM with weather and next-week features) but only 25% better than one factor, and worse in 2 of 14 years. The selection is not independent.
5. Anomalies and percentiles ignore the interval. District and union numbers are apportioned, not estimated.
6. There is a lot of extra surface (globe, outlook, AOD, RSS/CAP, sonification), and the team's own `SCIENTIFIC_REVIEW.md` lists most of the caveats above. That is honest, but judges will read it.
7. There is no software licence ("not yet selected"), so we cannot borrow code, only ideas with credit.

## 6. Best idea to borrow

**A per-week source flag plus conformal 80% bands, with coverage reported by scale.** Every unit-week carries `source in {observed, estimated, bridged}` and `lo80/hi80`, and the published coverage table is broken down by region-week, national-week and annual scales. Combine that with their **ARIA-grid calendar** pattern (keyboard-navigable heatmap with a table fallback). We should go further: propagate the band into "probability above median", and run a strictly nested calibration and an outer holdout, so that we beat them on the exact point they concede.
