# CEDRUS: strategy (v2)

_v2, 31 Oct 2026. Revised after the adversarial review. Built from eight research reports, which are kept in this folder._

## The pitch

**"MODIS is going dark. We built a fire record that survives the switch to newer satellites, and we tested it honestly, including where it fails."**

1. **The record breaks.** Join VIIRS onto MODIS naively and fire activity jumps about 2.7× in 2012. Suomi NPP stopped delivering data on 2 Nov 2026. Terra now passes at about 09:00 instead of 10:30, and Aqua at about 15:15 instead of 13:35.
2. **We fix it the way the instruments work.** For each fire VIIRS saw, we estimate how MODIS would have recorded it. That estimate is learned from thousands of near-simultaneous Aqua and Suomi NPP passes, and it depends on the fire's power. It is physics-informed and learned from the satellites' own data.
3. **We tell you how far to trust it.** Every month comes with a range. Each area gets a trust check: *"our correction error is small or large compared with normal year-to-year variation."* We show where our method and MODIS itself go wrong (the drift chart).

## What we do that the field doesn't

| Judges look for | Field's gap (eight strongest repos read line by line) | CEDRUS |
|---|---|---|
| A working demo on judging day | About half have dead backends, 404s or no deployment | Static site, no backend, cold load under 5 s |
| Valid science | Most validation is in-sample or leaks: tuned on test years, model picked on reported folds | Rules frozen first; model chosen in development; pass/fail on sealed regions; a drift-proof satellite-swap test |
| Use of fire physics | Detection-vs-power curves are shown but never used to harmonize | The learned detection response *is* the harmonizer |
| Uncertainty | Intervals exist, but anomaly calls ignore them | Every anomaly is graded against our own uncertainty (trust check) |
| Surviving the end of Suomi NPP | Most use Suomi NPP only | NOAA-20 curve; NOAA-21 bridged to NOAA-20; satellites averaged (as GFED5 does) |
| CSV | Mostly aggregate tables | Hotspots for the drawn area, each with its harmonization weight, so the CSV adds up exactly to the calendar |
| Honesty about MODIS drift | Usually ignored | A drift-divergence chart: what really happened to MODIS from 2022 |

## Regions
- **Development:** Portugal and the Eastern Mediterranean (Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Israel/Palestine, Cyprus, southern Turkey, Greece).
- **Sealed confirmation:** Morocco, Tunisia, central Chile and South Africa's Western Cape. None is opened until the model is frozen.
- **Shown in the app:**
  - Portugal is the default view (busy, good data);
  - Lebanon is our local story;
  - Chilecito, La Rioja, July 2026 is a one-click example: the challenge authors' own case, near real-time and labelled provisional.

## Awards targeted
**Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data and Local Impact.**

Judges score Impact, Creativity, Validity (which includes usability), Relevance (NASA data as the core) and Presentation, each 1–5. The written project page counts most. The demo is a video of 30 seconds or less **or** at most 7 slides, never both.

## Honesty rules (from the review)
- Don't claim "the first fire calendar for Lebanon". GWIS has country profiles, and competitor apps include Lebanon. Say instead: "a harmonized calendar for Lebanon, with a trust check on every month."
- Say "learned detection response, physics-informed", not "physics model".
- Don't claim errors in published papers.
- **Pre-event work:** the Beirut Local Lead approved starting early. Get that approval in an email, and **disclose all pre-event work on the project page**, with the commit history as evidence.
