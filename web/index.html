    The problem

    New satellitessee more fires.That isn'tmore fire.

    VIIRS on Suomi NPP (from 2012) and NOAA-20 (from 2018) detect smaller fires than MODIS. Add Suomi NPP's burning days to the MODIS record as they are, and the years from 2012 look worse than they were.

    Naive merge · bar height = burning days

    The fix

    Harmonized toone MODIS-equivalent scale.

    We learn how VIIRS burning days translate into MODIS-equivalent burning days over 2012–2019, when MODIS and Suomi NPP fly together (NOAA-20 is tied in through Suomi NPP), and use it for every month from 2022, when the MODIS satellites drift. The false climb flattens. Real fire years still stand out: ….

    TWO EYES, ONE FIRE

    Why raw satellite counts jump in 2012, and how the record fixes it

    The same fire, seen by two satellite eyes. MODIS sees in pixels about 1 km wide; VIIRS (from 2012) in pixels 375 m wide, so about seven of its pixels fit in one of MODIS’s, and it also catches smaller, cooler fires. Counted raw, the sharper eye makes fire look as if it jumped overnight. The record translates every count into MODIS-equivalent terms, so the whole record sits on one scale.

    Like a student sitting an exam: we wrote the rules and the pass marks down, with the date, before downloading each exam's regions, then let the model sit it once. The first one failed, and we published that. Each of the next two passed every test.

    Data & Methodology

    Three steps from
    detection to calendar.

    Step 1 · Detect

    Read every fire pixel

    Active-fire detections from NASA FIRMS: MODIS Collection 6.1 on Terra and Aqua since 2000, VIIRS 375 m on Suomi NPP since 2012 and NOAA-20 since 2018. Vegetation fires only (type 0); MODIS confidence ≥ 30; VIIRS confidence nominal or high.

    Step 2 · Harmonize

    Put VIIRS on MODIS's scale

    A model fitted over 2012–2019, when MODIS and Suomi NPP overlap, converts VIIRS records into MODIS-equivalent burning days (or detections), per 0.25° cell, calendar month and day or night. NOAA-20 is tied in through Suomi NPP. Each estimate carries an 80% range; a test showed those ranges are too narrow.

    Step 3 · Aggregate

    Count burning days per cell

    Each ~1 km square counts once per day it burns. Squares roll up into 0.25° cells and any shape you draw, month by month. Up to June 2002 the record is bridged from Terra alone; July 2002 to 2021 is observed MODIS; from 2022 it is estimated from VIIRS.

    MODIS · Terra, Aqua1 kmfire pixel at nadir · 2,330 km swath · 705 km orbit
    VIIRS · Suomi NPP, NOAA-20375 mfire pixel · 3,040 km swath · 824 km orbit
    Overlap used for fitting2012–2019MODIS with Suomi NPP; NOAA-20 via Suomi NPP, 2018–2019

    What this record is not

    • Neither unit is burned area: a burning day says a ~1 km square had a fire detected that day, not how much of it burned.
    • Values from 2022 are MODIS-equivalent estimates, not observations; their 80% ranges are approximate and often too narrow.
    • Blank does not mean fire-free: monthly observation completeness is unknown.

    About

    Roots in nature.
    Data for a safer tomorrow.

    CEDRUS is named for the cedar of Lebanon, a tree that has outlived centuries of fire and drought. We are joining the NASA Space Apps local event in Beirut.

    Our challenge: Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots. MODIS is being retired, and Suomi NPP stops delivering data on 2 Nov 2026. We build one fire record that survives the handover, test it honestly, and turn it into a fire calendar for any area, so a trend on the screen reflects what happened on the ground.

    Use of AI. AI coding assistants helped write the pipeline and this website. The method, the test rules and every number shown come from the code and data in the repository and can be rebuilt from it.
